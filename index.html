Three taps to a new contact

No pairing, no Bluetooth dance, no “what's your number again?”

  1. Build your card

    Add your details once, split into Professional and Personal. It's stored on your phone only.

  2. Choose what to share

    Pick a preset for this person. Your QR code carries only those fields, nothing extra.

  3. Scan, sort, save

    They scan your code with their camera, drop you into a group like “AI CEO Course”, add a note, and keep you.

Share exactly what you mean to

Four presets cover the usual cases. A single “include phone numbers” switch overrides all of them, even Everything.

Your network, organised

Group the people you meet by event, team or topic, search them later, and keep a note on each.

Save to your phone's contacts

Any contact exports as a standard .vcf file. Your phone's own “Add to Contacts” sheet finishes the job, and you stay in control of it.

No server. Really.

That's the whole point, so here is exactly what it means.

No account

There's nothing to sign up for and nobody to log in as. Open it and go.

Works offline

The QR code is drawn and read on your phone. Both of you can be in airplane mode.

Stays on your device

Your card and the people you collect live in your browser's storage. They are never uploaded.

No tracking

No analytics, no ads, and no network calls from the app itself.

The trade-off: because nothing is synced, your data lives only on that one phone's browser. Clearing site data or switching phones means starting fresh, so export contacts you care about to your phone's address book.

Put it on your home screen

Parichay is a web app, so there's no app store. Open it once while you're online, add it to your home screen, and it launches like any other app.

iPhone (Safari)

  1. Open parichay.jagdeepsinghvirdi.com/app in Safari.
  2. Tap the Share button.
  3. Choose Add to Home Screen, then Add.

Android (Chrome)

  1. Open parichay.jagdeepsinghvirdi.com/app in Chrome.
  2. Tap the ⋮ menu.
  3. Choose Install app (or Add to Home screen).

Camera access: to scan someone's card, Parichay asks for your camera. The video is processed on your phone and never recorded or sent anywhere.
